Phase 4 is here: the Z.ai chat engine is now a fully autonomous agent. A new "Autonomy" control on the Z.ai tab chooses how much the engine may do: Off (chat and files only), Ask each (every command waits for your approval), Safe auto (read-only commands run on their own, everything else asks), or Full auto (commands run on their own inside the private Linux workspace - this is the default). When the loop is enabled, Z.ai's replies can carry RUN: commands; they execute in your project's workspace and the results flow back into the same conversation until the task reports DONE. You watch everything live in the chat: a collapsible "Commands run" activity log (per command a check/cross/clock marker and a redacted summary), an approval card when commands wait for you, and a final report card (rounds, commands, refusals, why it stopped, the summary). Safety: hard rails refuse destructive commands without asking (rm -rf outside the workspace, force pushes, history rewrites, deleting remote repos/databases, system commands, fork bombs, curl|sh, printing env or token values), with bypass hardening (segmentation on shell operators, quoting normalization, base64-into-shell). Token values reach commands ONLY as environment variables (git pushes use a process-scoped credential helper - nothing is written to .git/config or URLs) and command output is redacted + tail-bounded before it returns to chat.z.ai. A file/web/command-output untrusted-data rule is taught to the model; budgets cap the loop (default 40 rounds, per-command 120s timeout, total-output cap, no-progress detection). Also fixed in this build: a latent crash in credential-helper assembly caught by the new tests, and a budget-message formatting bug. The loop never touches app startup and is fully crash-safe.
